Picture a book lying open — content on the left-hand page, a picture or video on the right-hand page, side by side, both taking up roughly equal space. That's the whole idea of this block, and it's genuinely different from image-text-section: nothing wraps around anything here, it's a clean two-column split.

The HTML

<div class="flex-section">
  <div class="text-content">
    (your content goes here)
  </div>
  <div class="video-content">
    <iframe src="..."></iframe>
  </div>
</div>

The CSS

display: flex on the outer wrapper lines the two inner boxes up side by side. flex: 1 on both of them means "split the available space evenly between us" — like cutting a sandwich exactly in half, regardless of how wide the page actually is.

On phones, flex-direction: column switches that same layout from side-by-side to stacked — the same two boxes, just rearranged vertically once the screen gets too narrow for two columns to sit comfortably next to each other.

Why media is required here

Unlike image-text-section, there's no "None" option for the right column — an empty second column wouldn't make sense in a layout whose entire point is two columns side by side.

The "Another content block" option

Picking this for the right column doesn't generate any media at all — it just leaves a marker instead:

<div class="video-content">
  <!-- CONTENT BLOCK GOES HERE -->
</div>

Same idea as the placeholder used in accordion and icon-toggle panels: build whatever you actually need on another tab (buttons, for instance) and paste its generated HTML in place of that comment. This block only ever handles the two-column structure itself — it doesn't try to generate every possible thing that could go in the right column.
