In 30 seconds

Text that wraps around a picture or video, like a photo in a newspaper column. The media is told to float to one side, and the text flows around it, then underneath once it passes the bottom of the picture.

The media is optional: with none, you just get your text.

See it on your own block. Open the Explain tab beside the preview, turn on X-ray, and hover the image: the code line for the picture lights up and tells you about float.

The idea: float

Picture a newspaper column with a photo tucked into the corner and the text flowing around it, wrapping underneath once it runs past the photo's height. That is exactly what this block does.

<div class="image-text-section">
  <img src="..." alt="..." />
  <div>
    (your content goes here)
  </div>
</div>

Two things sit inside the wrapper: the media, then a div holding whatever you wrote. Whatever you used (headings, bullet points, plain paragraphs) is carried through as real HTML.

Why the order matters

The media comes first in the code. A floated picture can only sit beside text that comes after it, so putting it first is what lets the text wrap round it. Move it below the text and it would drop underneath instead.

The "Large" size

Choosing "Large" adds a class, media-large, which makes the picture bigger (340px rather than 275px). That is how the old "badge" style, such as the pledge certificate graphic, was folded into this block.

On phones

There is not room to put things side by side on a small screen. The CSS switches the float off and shows the picture full width above the text instead.

Deep dive: floats and the "clearfix"

float: right is an old but very useful CSS trick. It pulls the image out to the right-hand side and lets the text in the neighbouring div flow around it like water round a rock.

The catch: a floated element no longer counts towards its parent's height, so it can spill out of its own box and overlap whatever comes next. This rule stops that:

.image-text-section::after {
  content: "";
  display: block;
  clear: both;
}

It adds an invisible block at the end of the wrapper that refuses to sit beside any float. That forces the wrapper to grow tall enough to contain the picture. It is called a "clearfix", and you will see it in plenty of CSS.
