Picture a stack of business cards laid out in a neat grid — each one clickable, each one the same height as its neighbours in the same row, however short or long its title happens to be.

The HTML

<div class="card">
  <img src="..." class="resource-card__img" />
  <div>
    <a href="..." class="stretched-link" target="_blank">
      <strong>Title text <i class="fa-solid fa-up-right-from-square"></i></strong>
    </a>
    <p><strong>Type</strong><span>Duration</span></p>
  </div>
</div>

The stretched-link class is a neat CSS trick: it stretches the clickable area of that link invisibly to cover the entire card, even though the actual link text is only a few words. That's why clicking anywhere on the card — the image, the empty space, not just the title — opens the link.

Why every card in a row matches height

By default, a grid lets each item size itself to fit its own content — so a card with a two-line title would end up shorter than one with a three-line title next to it. Telling the grid to stretch instead makes every card fill the full height of its row, and then the card's own layout (a flexbox column with justify-content: space-between) pushes the type/duration line down to the bottom either way — like a bookend holding the price tag at the bottom of the shelf no matter how thick the book is.

The new-tab checkbox

Ticking it adds target="_blank" and the little arrow icon, tied to the one checkbox so neither can be forgotten. The builder also adds rel="noopener noreferrer" (a safety measure for links that open a new tab) but only to external links. Relative links and links to our own domains (listed in INTERNAL_HOSTS) keep just target="_blank".
